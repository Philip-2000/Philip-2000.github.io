### 2026-10-27 · Suzhou

So much has happened this year. From Beijing to Shenzhen, then Shanghai, and finally Suzhou, each move brought an entirely different kind of work. It almost tore me apart. Along the way, I also came face to face with my own limitations: little experience training models, a muddled approach to getting things done, an undeveloped sense of research taste, and a pace that was never quite fast enough. Before graduating, I had anticipated that I might not be capable enough, but it was only a vague notion then. Having reality confirm it was still deeply painful.

My teacher GPT said, “What you are really wrestling with is whether your abilities are fundamentally lacking, or whether you have yet to find the right path for your development.” It also said, “Resources can have a profound effect on outcomes. Failure should not be attributed entirely to a person's abilities.”

“Five years is a long time—long enough that you ought to be able to accomplish much. Five years is also a short time—too short to define your life.”

Then what should define it? All I can say is that continuing my studies through a PhD is a path I have consciously chosen out of interest, rather than simply drifting along some supposedly predetermined track. Nor do my past experiences mean that I am destined never to accomplish anything. I am simply packing my bags and setting out again.

Finally, I want to thank AI, especially agents. Last October, I began using a coding agent that could work beyond the chat window—GitHub Copilot—but I did not trust it enough at the time. It was not until late May this year that I really began giving Codex room to work. By July, I had connected it to Feishu, bringing office work, communication, and presentations together. By August, I was using it on my phone, giving voice instructions through the ChatGPT app. The seamless experience of having “an entire universe at my fingertips” far surpassed the amazement I felt when conversational AI first appeared. Only then did it feel as though productivity had truly entered a new era.

This brings new questions, too. In this era, the kinds of questions graduate students need to answer have changed, the skills they need to prioritize have shifted, and the standards for results are rising sharply. I need to adapt deliberately and keep pace with the times if I am to avoid repeating the mistakes of the past few years.

Of course, my conversations with my AI teachers have ranged far beyond this. Throughout the year, we have talked about class, finance, politics, management, history, aesthetics, and much more. Through these conversations, I have gradually learned to resist being swept along by a single narrative, to look more closely at how society works, and to appreciate both the surprises and the weight of history. I have begun to rethink what I am pursuing: happiness in life, or being better than other people. I once thought the two were one and the same. Yet even that supposed “unity” was never well defined.

I am grateful to live in an age with AI. (Translated by ChatGPT)

### 2026-03-08 · Shenzhen

In the acknowledgments of my master's thesis, after thanking my advisor and senior, I wrote the last paragraph as this:
>"Finally, I will not thank myself. Because this fledgling eagle has flown into my dreams many times. It sits by the lotus pond, what is it fantasizing about? Perhaps fantasizing about the sky being filled with delivery drones, perhaps fantasizing about every household having robots to take care of their lives, perhaps fantasizing about everyone being able to fly into the canyon with VR glasses to listen to the sound of destiny's breaking. I don't know, I can only stand quietly behind that eagle, afraid that it will turn around and see me and great to me. Just because that, at this moment, there are too many questions that cannot be answered, too many expectations that are difficult to fulfill, and too many doubts that cannot be responded to. I also don't want to make up reasons, I'll just agree to regrets with my silence. However, I'm grateful that the road ahead is not over, and I'll keep my head down and way up. Maybe, the answer to the questions, are still ahead..."

This paragraph exactly fits as the content of my personal homepage.
